How knee candidates are selected

A sampled knee, with the evidence shown

The 90% rule identifies a sampled throughput knee proxy. The diagrams show throughput (T), percent of sampled peak, p95 TTFT and the neighboring throughput/latency change. Dotted horizontal lines on throughput charts show the 90% thresholds. A knee is unconfirmed when the lower range is missing or the curve still needs higher-concurrency samples. This is not a fitted physical knee or a latency-SLO guarantee.

TTFT charts mark the same throughput-based concurrency. They show what latency you get at that candidate, rather than claiming an independently estimated latency knee.

How to read these results

Audit complete; model calibration remains open

All 55 samples completed a full 3,600-second profiling window after warmup. Checks cover replay configuration, dependency structure, tokenizer agreement, request accounting, timeouts and worker placement. Agg also checks replay inputs against the saved hardware runs.

All three agg charts use the same eight matched policy/concurrency samples; RR at 192 and 384 reuses the capacity-control runs. Both disagg charts use all 47 samples from the 64-GPU topology sweep. Agg and disagg use different cache-capacity assumptions. The fixed-rate service and approximate cache models still have known hardware gaps; hybrid/Mamba state and shared GPU scheduling are not modeled here.
